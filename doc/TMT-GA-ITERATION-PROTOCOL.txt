TMT G/A-only iteration and adversarial audit — frozen before iteration-1 scoring
2026-11-11

User request: preserve terminal K tags, filter only the G/A pseudo-residue
patterns, benchmark both configured and unconfigured TMT masses, iterate to
improve detection, and critically assess failure cases and the reference truth.

Iteration 1
Remove the terminal bare-K rejection only. Retain the N/K two-site detector,
TMTpro abstention, mass windows, sequence conditions, preprocessing, scores,
and pre-dedup/pre-cap placement. Do not activate the separate reporter filter.
The same four TMT files and label-free control, unchanged Sage references and
chemistry configurations, are used with the original frozen scoring harness.
Compare baseline, new default/off, forced G/A on, and automatic G/A filtering.
Three serial, randomly ordered repetitions per cell, eight threads on dax.
9 cells x 4 arms x 3 repetitions = 108 runs. Record load and elapsed runtime.
Use a new output directory and unique binary name; retain the original results.

Primary outcomes: recall@50 and rank-1 accuracy under the existing sequence
plus one-correct-flank rule. Supplement: strict residue-mass consistency.
Denominators remain all reference PSMs; no output remains a miss.
Desired improvement: rank-1 gains without losses of primary recall, separately
for label-blind and label-aware settings. Report paired gains AND losses,
independent validation projects separately from development/same-project data.

Audit and iteration policy
Inspect every newly lost correct call, if any. Quantify remaining recall and
ranking failures, then examine selected spectra from defined failure strata.
Check source/native-ID mapping, precursor mass/charge/isotopes, modification
parsing, fragment evidence, score/q-value, isobaric ambiguities, and independent
search-engine agreement where available. A q-value is a set-level error-rate
estimate, not proof of a particular peptide. Engine agreement is supporting
evidence, not absolute truth; disagreements remain uncertain without evidence.
Do not delete or relabel inconvenient references to improve headline metrics.
Keep any adjudicated/consensus subset or alternative valid peptides separate.

Test automatic label gating on additional existing positive/negative inputs
where feasible, including TMTpro and unsupported acquisition modes. Distinguish
abstention from false activation and distinguish label detection from tag
sequence detection. Preserve expectations before inspecting decisions.

If a reproducible defect warrants another change, document its mechanism and
freeze the changed rule before the next benchmark. Report every iteration,
including failures. Reusing these validation files for tuning makes later
results exploratory; do not describe them as untouched held-out validation.
Stop when a reproducible improvement is demonstrated and remaining failure
types are explained to the level supported by the data, or when available
evidence cannot justify a further change. Do not tune indefinitely to a finite
reference set or claim that every residual failure has been solved.

Iteration 2 — conflicting-label veto, after frozen iteration-1 results
The initial 90:10 TMT:TMTpro mixture produced 29 N-site and 16 K-site
304-Da supports. The shared minimum of 20 therefore failed to veto it.
The eight original TMT chemistry panels and four additional high-resolution
TMT files had at most 2 N-site and 1 K-site 304-Da supports. This motivates a
separate conflict threshold, without weakening positive TMT evidence.

The candidate rule was frozen before executing the second set of mixtures:
keep eligible >=100 and the positive threshold max(20,ceil(eligible/100));
veto only when BOTH 304-Da site counts reach max(5,ceil(eligible/100)). Keep
sampling, mass windows, G/A rejection, K retention, and Da abstention unchanged.
The veto reason is conflicting_304Da_label_evidence: these mass anchors are
compatible with both TMTpro and iTRAQ8 and do not identify the chemistry.

Second-source validation used TMT PXD021560 and TMTpro PXD039509, separate
from the initial mixture sources, with sampling seed 1948 and order seed 403.
The source files' pure counts had already been inspected; the new mixture
outcomes had not. These are artificial mixtures of independently acquired MS2
spectra, not chemical dilutions or co-isolation experiments, and are a small
mechanistic validation rather than an untouched population-level test.
The unchanged iteration-1 binary supplied counts for an offline rule replay:
